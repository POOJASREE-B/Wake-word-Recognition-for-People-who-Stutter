# Literature notes: what was read and what it changed in this project

Only papers that were actually read (full text or abstract + conclusion, as stated) are listed. Nothing here is quoted from memory.

| Paper | What it reports (as read) | What this project took from it |
|---|---|---|
| Mitra et al., *Analysis and Tuning of a Voice Assistant System for Dysfluent Speech*, Apple, Interspeech 2021, arXiv:2106.11759 (read: full text; citation checked 2026-10-31) | A consumer voice assistant degrades on people who stutter: insertion errors are more than 13x higher; repetitions tend to occur at the beginning of utterances. Tuning (language model, end-pointing) helps. | Stutter simulation puts events at the first voiced segment with a measured probability (`p_initial`); the live page waits longer before rejecting (REJECT_TAIL_S 1.2 s) so a block is not cut off. |
| Zhang et al., *Stutter-TTS*, Amazon Alexa AI, NeurIPS 2022 workshop, arXiv:2211.09731 (read: full text; citation checked) | Table 1: phoneme repetition 40.11%, dysrhythmic phonation 21.40%, block 15.59% of utterances. Mixing 3% synthetic stuttered data gave a 5.74% relative WER reduction on stuttered speech with 0.18% relative degradation on fluent speech. | Synthetic disfluency is kept a minority of training data and is always mixed with real recordings (P_DISFL = 0.35 on real voices); the set of simulated types (prolongation, repetition, block) follows this taxonomy. |
| *Summary of the Low-Resource Dysarthria Wake-Up Word Spotting Challenge*, SLT 2024 (read: abstract + conclusion; **author list and arXiv ID NOT verified**: the paper turned up in a Google Scholar query only, so confirm it on [lrdwws.org](https://www.lrdwws.org/) before citing) | 25 teams, 7 submitted. Winning ideas: dual filter (wake-word-spotting probability combined with ASR output), pretrained self-supervised features, cosine-similarity prototype classification, TTS augmentation. | The detector already uses a pretrained speech embedding and a second, independent decision layer (decoder grammar + verifier). Prototype/enrolment is NOT implemented; it is the main literature-backed idea left. |
| Liu et al., *Optimizing Dysarthria Wake-Up Word Spotting: An End-to-End Approach for SLT 2024 LRDWWS Challenge*, arXiv:2409.10076 (read: abstract + conclusion; ID checked) | Pretrained data2vec2 with joint ASR + wake-word multi-task fine-tuning, dual-filter strategy and TTS augmentation: FAR 0.00321, FRR 0.005 on the challenge evaluation set (Mandarin dysarthric speech, large training corpus). | Supports the ensemble of a pretrained embedding with the task model. Their numbers are for a different language, disorder and training-set size: not comparable with ours. |
| Wang et al., *PB-LRDWWS System for the SLT 2024 LRDWWS Challenge*, arXiv:2409.04799 (read: abstract + conclusion; ID checked) | Fine-tuned HuBERT features of the target speaker's enrolment speech form prototypes; classification by cosine similarity; 2nd place. Speaker-dependent models helped. | Motivates a per-user enrolment step (not built). |
| Mujtaba et al., *Inclusive ASR for Disfluent Speech*, Interspeech 2024, arXiv:2406.10177, and *Fine-Tuning ASR for Stuttered Speech: Personalized vs. Generalized*, Interspeech 2025, arXiv:2506.00853 (read: abstracts; IDs and venues checked) | Fine-tuning a self-supervised model on a small curated stuttered set plus disfluency augmentation reduces ASR error; personalised models are compared with generalised ones. | Supports training on a small real set plus augmentation, and supports personalisation as a next step. |
| Lea et al., SEP-28k, arXiv:2102.12394 (read earlier: label statistics) | Event annotations of podcasts; block and prolongation labels have low annotator agreement. | Block and prolongation counts in `stutter_top/pattern_report.md` are treated as lower bounds with low reliability. |

## Honest limits
- None of these systems wake on "Hey Google"; they target ASR or Mandarin dysarthric speech. No result here is a benchmark for this project.
- Datasets used in this project and their terms are listed in the licensing review (SEP-28k labels CC BY-NC 4.0 with no audio licence; FluencyBank under the TalkBank Ground Rules; TORGO academic, non-profit use). All of them restrict this work to non-commercial research and the audio cannot be redistributed. The 23 real speakers' recordings need documented consent and, for most venues, ethics approval or exemption.
- UCLASS and the LRDWWS Mandarin corpus (CDSD) were not used.
